Scholio Score

One number a student can improve and a university can trust

The Scholio Score is a 0 to 100 measure of scholarship readiness for Master's and PhD applicants. It is deterministic, its weights are public, and every score carries an ID that reproduces it. Nothing about it is hidden.

Why it can be trusted
DeterministicSame inputs, same score, any device, any day. Version 1.0 is frozen; changes get a new version number.
Reproducible by IDEach score ID (for example SS-1K7Q3ZP) is derived from the answers. Anyone with the answers can recompute it.
Separate from eligibilityThe score measures readiness. Eligibility (nationality, age) is checked per award and never inflates the number.
Verified tier (planned)With accounts, students will upload transcripts and test reports; a verified score will carry a checkmark universities and funders can query by ID.
How each component is computed (v1.0)
  • Academics: GPA on a 4.0 scale, linear from 2.5 (0) to 4.0 (100).
  • Research: publications or conference papers; Master's starts at 20 with +40 per paper, PhD starts at 0 with +30 per paper, capped at 100.
  • Experience: 20 points per year of relevant work or research, capped at 5 years.
  • English: 0 with no test; linear from IELTS 5.5 (0) to 7.5 (100). TOEFL and PTE map to IELTS bands.
  • Readiness: 25 points per document stage (CV, statement, referees) plus 25 for a supervisor or offer.
  • Leadership: 15 (none), 55 (led something), 90 (led with measurable results).
For universities and funders

A Scholio Score tells you in one glance whether an applicant has the academic record, language, research base and readiness your award expects, before you open the file. Because the method is public you can audit it, and because it is separate from eligibility it does not overstate anyone.

Sample 1

Scholarship motivation letter (Master's, fully funded)

Dear Selection Committee,

I am applying for the Excellence Scholarship to join the MSc in Environmental Engineering in September 2027. I want to build flood early-warning tools that work in places without gauging stations, and the Water Resources group is the only one I have found that publishes open rainfall-runoff models for ungauged Himalayan catchments.

In 2026 I built and ran a bilingual flood bulletin for a district hit by a glacial-lake outburst. It reached 40,000 people in its first week and was adopted by the district administration. The alerts were calibrated by hand from upstream reports; the limit I hit was hydrological modelling, and that is the gap this degree closes.

My undergraduate thesis (first class, 3.7/4.0) modelled sediment transport in a monsoon river using open satellite data. The method was crude but it taught me to work with sparse, noisy inputs, which is the normal case for the communities I want to serve.

I would use the two-year programme to validate the group's model on my district's data, which I already hold with local partners, and to turn the bulletin into a tool other districts can run themselves. Within five years I intend to return to the public sector at home, where I have already worked for three years, and lead the national rollout.

Thank you for considering my application.

Sincerely,
A. Applicant

Why it works
  • Direction in the first two sentences, with a named group.
  • Two concrete episodes with numbers (40,000 people; 3.7 GPA).
  • The gap is stated plainly, and the degree is the answer to it.
  • A five-year plan the funder can picture. 280 words.
Sample 2

First email to a prospective PhD supervisor

Subject: PhD enquiry: low-cost flood sensing in ungauged catchments (Fall 2027)

Dear Dr. Lin,

I read your 2025 paper on transfer learning for rainfall-runoff prediction in data-sparse basins. I am writing because I have a dataset it could be tested on, and I would like to do that as a PhD student in your group.

I hold a BEng in Civil Engineering (3.7/4.0) and for the past three years I have worked in my country's ministry of finance on digital public infrastructure. In 2026 I built a flood bulletin for a district with no gauging station; it now holds 14 months of crowd-reported water levels for 23 sites, which I have permission to share for research.

My question: would your group be open to a PhD project that applies your transfer-learning approach to this kind of citizen-reported data? If so, I would apply through the Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme by 1 December and would welcome ten minutes of your advice on framing the proposal.

My CV and a one-page proposal sketch are attached.

Kind regards,
A. Applicant
[phone] · [LinkedIn]

Why it works
  • Subject line names the topic and the intake.
  • Opens with their paper and what you bring (a dataset), not with flattery.
  • One clear question with a date attached.
  • Under 200 words; the attachments carry the detail.
Sample 3

Reference request to a former professor

Dear Professor Rai,

I am applying for the Gates Cambridge Scholarship (deadline 8 December) for the MPhil in Technology Policy, and I would be grateful if you would write one of my two references.

You supervised my final-year thesis on sediment transport in 2023 and taught me Hydrology II, where I scored 92%. If you are able to write, I will send a one-page summary of what I have done since, the scholarship's criteria, and the portal link; the reference is submitted online and takes about 30 minutes.

If your schedule does not allow it this cycle, I completely understand and would appreciate a quick reply so I can ask someone else in time.

With thanks,
A. Applicant

Why it works
  • Names the award, the deadline and the course in line one.
  • Reminds them who you are with specifics.
  • Promises to make it easy: summary, criteria, link, time estimate.
  • Gives them a graceful way to say no, early.
Rules for all three
First sentence does the jobAward, course, intake, or the one question you are asking. Never "I hope this finds you well".
One number per paragraphGrades, people reached, months of data, team size. Numbers are read; adjectives are skipped.
Name the gapWhat you cannot yet do, and why this degree or person fixes it. Panels trust applicants who know their limits.
Stop at 300 wordsMotivation letters 250 to 300; emails under 200. Attachments carry detail.
FormattingPlain text or a one-page PDF. Your name in the filename. No photos unless the funder asks.

IELTS Writing Task 2 · sample with model answer

Prompt (40 minutes, at least 250 words): Some people believe that governments should fund university education for all students. Others think students should pay for their own studies. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer (band 7+ features marked)

Whether the state or the student should pay for a degree is a question every country answers differently. This essay examines the case for public funding and the case for personal payment before arguing that a mixed model serves societies best.

Supporters of free university education point to access. When fees are abolished, as in Germany and Norway, enrolment among lower-income families rises, and the economy gains graduates who would otherwise never have applied. They also note that the public already benefits from the doctors, engineers and teachers that universities produce, so it is reasonable for the public to pay.

Those who favour student payment reply that free places are not free: taxpayers who never attend university end up subsidising those who will later earn more. They argue that fees make students choose courses carefully and complete them faster, and that income-contingent loans, as in Australia, protect graduates who earn little.

In my view, neither extreme is satisfactory. Full public funding is costly and can crowd out schools and hospitals; full private payment deters precisely the students a country most needs to reach. A model in which tuition is state-funded for a capped number of years, with loans repaid only above an income threshold, captures the benefits of both.

In conclusion, while both positions have merit, a blended system that removes the upfront barrier yet asks successful graduates to contribute is the fairest and most sustainable choice. (268 words)

What earns the band
  • Clear position stated in the introduction and held throughout.
  • Each body paragraph has one idea, an example, and a link back to the question.
  • Range: "income-contingent", "crowd out", "capped"; complex sentences without errors.
  • Cohesion through reference ("They also note", "reply that") rather than listing linkers.
IELTS Reading · mini passage with questions

Passage (extract, 7 minutes): Early-warning systems for floods have traditionally depended on networks of river gauges. These instruments are accurate but expensive to install and maintain, and in mountainous regions many are destroyed by the very events they are meant to record. A newer approach replaces or supplements gauges with reports from residents, submitted by phone. Such "citizen observations" are less precise, yet because they arrive from many points along a river, they can reveal the shape of a flood wave in a way a single gauge cannot. Researchers in Nepal and Bangladesh found that combining the two sources cut false alarms by a third without reducing the number of real events detected.

Speaking Part 2 · cue card and a model structure

Cue card (1 minute to prepare, speak for 2): Describe a project you worked on that you are proud of. You should say what it was, who you worked with, what your role was, and explain why you are proud of it.

Set the scene (20 s)"The project I want to talk about is a flood bulletin I built last year, during the monsoon, for a district north of the capital."
Who and what (40 s)Team size, your role, two concrete tasks. Use past tenses accurately; one conditional ("if we had waited, …").
The difficulty (30 s)One problem and how you solved it. Examiners score this for fluency and range.
Why proud (30 s)A result with a number, and one sentence about what you learned.

About

Every scholarship, within reach

Scholio is an independent, global index of Master's and PhD funding. The information that decides whether a talented student can afford to study is scattered across hundreds of university pages, embassy notices and social posts, and most of it goes stale the week it is written. We fix that.

Verified, then published

Every listing is checked against its official page, dated, and re-checked every 30 days. If we cannot verify it, it does not go live.

One template for everything

Level, fields, deadline, funding, English test, admission-first, who may apply. The same shape for a government award and a university fellowship.

Honest reviews only

Only people who marked "applied" or "awarded" can rate an award. No funder can edit or remove a review.

Your data stays yours

The fit score runs in your browser first. Sharing with a funder is opt-in. Export or delete everything, any time.

Not an agent

Not a consultancy, not an application portal. You always apply on the funder's own site. We never charge seekers to search.

Free for seekers, always

Revenue comes from paid Academy intensives, funder plans and university partnerships. Funders never buy a rating or a search position.
